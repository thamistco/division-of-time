# Division of Time

A calm, evidence-based time tracker for a GP trainee who is also a parent. Linked to Google Calendar.

**Open it:** https://thamistco.github.io/division-of-time/

## What it does
- **One-tap tracking** with GP trainee categories (surgery, results & letters, OOH, tutorial, self-study, ePortfolio, family…); keys 1–9, Space, F, A, ←/→, Ctrl K command menu, ? for help
- **Plan vs actual**: Google Calendar beside what really happened; log clinics and meetings in one tap; striped untracked gaps to fill
- **Training week**: clinical vs education hours against the COGPED 70:30 week (28h + 12h, pro rata for LTFT), admin-to-clinical ratio against the BMA's 1 : 3 guide, OOH hours this month
- **Your 168 hours**: Laura Vanderkam-style week grid in 30-minute cells
- **Focus sessions** (25/50/90) with a focus rating and a "next step" note; deep-work heatmap and best window
- **Shutdown review**: day rating, "felt in control" rating, learning-log counter, loose ends, tomorrow's one big thing (sent to Google Calendar)
- **Weekly review**: wins, what to drop, next week's top three, one big and one little adventure
- Goals per day, week or month; forgotten-timer guard; undo on every delete; light/dark themes; works offline; installable

## Research behind it
Aeon et al. 2021 (time management and wellbeing), Claessens et al. 2007, Harkin et al. 2016, Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, Masicampo & Baumeister 2011, Buehler et al. 1994, Leroy 2009, Mark et al. 2005/2008, Sharif et al. 2021, Whillans et al. 2017, COGPED training-week guidance, BMA safe-working guidance, GP Worklife Survey, GMC National Training Survey 2025, Vanderkam's 168 Hours. Design: WCAG 2.2 contrast, Okabe–Ito colour logic, Few/Tufte on charts.

Data stays in your browser (localStorage). Export a JSON backup any time.
